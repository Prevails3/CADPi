Arizona Surveillance Law: Evidentiary Admissibility in Maricopa County Courts

JD
John Donofrio
Operations Manager & Private Investigator, CAD PI

Physical surveillance remains the most definitive evidentiary asset in domestic infidelity disputes, child custody hearings, personal injury defense, and corporate fraud litigation. However, a single statutory misstep can render hundreds of hours of high-definition video inadmissible under the Arizona Rules of Evidence—or worse, expose counsel and their clients to civil tort liability or criminal misdemeanor charges.

Operating in Maricopa County, Phoenix, and across Arizona demands absolute precision regarding statutory surveillance boundaries.

"Unassailable courtroom evidence requires both technical excellence in the field and strict adherence to Arizona privacy statutes. If evidence is gathered unlawfully, it is toxic to your case."

Arizona's One-Party Consent Rule (A.R.S. § 13-3005)

Arizona is statutory a "one-party consent" state for recording conversations under A.R.S. § 13-3005. This allows an individual to record an oral or wire communication provided at least one participating party consents.

The Critical Investigator Limitation: A private detective or client cannot plant an unattended audio recorder in a vehicle, office, or dining room where neither the investigator nor the client is physically present in the dialogue. Doing so constitutes felony eavesdropping. Video surveillance, however, operates under an entirely different legal standard: the constitutional test of reasonable expectation of privacy.

The Reasonable Expectation of Privacy Test (A.R.S. § 13-3019)

Under A.R.S. § 13-3019 (Surreptitious Photographing, Videotaping, and Filming), capturing imagery of a person without consent is illegal only if the subject is in an area where they hold an objective, reasonable expectation of privacy.

  • Public Domains: Any individual walking down Camelback Road, dining at a public outdoor patio in Scottsdale, or entering an office complex has no reasonable expectation of privacy. Visual evidence gathered from public easements is 100% lawful and admissible.
  • Private Curtilage: Aiming lenses into residential bedroom windows, using ladders or drones to peer over privacy perimeter walls, or trespassing into backyards violates Arizona law. Elite investigators position vehicles lawfully on public thoroughfares, capturing subjects only when they enter plain view.

GPS Tracking Restrictions (A.R.S. § 13-2923)

One of the most frequent questions from prospective clients is whether a magnetic GPS tracker can be placed on an unfaithful spouse's vehicle. Under Arizona stalking statutes (A.R.S. § 13-2923), installing a tracking device on a vehicle without title ownership is unlawful.

In Arizona, GPS tracking is legally permissible only if the client's name appears on the legal vehicle title or registration (such as marital joint property). If the vehicle is titled exclusively in the other party's name, GPS placement is strictly prohibited without a court order—making manual, discreet physical mobile surveillance the only legal recourse.

Best Practices for Surviving Motion in Limine Challenges
  • Preserve raw, unaltered native video files: Maintain digital master copies with embedded camera EXIF timestamps and SHA-256 hashes.
  • Maintain contemporaneous field logs: Every minute of surveillance must be recorded in an operational log detailing locations, weather, lighting, and investigator positioning.
  • Never use amateur GPS tracking: Tainted evidence can trigger severe judicial sanctions and compromise your entire legal strategy.
